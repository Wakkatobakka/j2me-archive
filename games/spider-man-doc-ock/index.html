Spider-Man: Doc Ock

The Spider-Man 2 film tie-in puts three chases and fights on a phone keypad.

About the game

Spider-Man runs on his own as you swing him between rooftops or fire webs. The second stage trades the city skyline for an oncoming subway train. The third asks you to web up Doc Ock’s arms. Once you have beaten the story stages, you can replay them in arcade mode.[1][2]

Development

Vicarious Visions’ Java credits name Eric DeSantis for design and art, Robert Gallerani for art, and four programmers. Shin’en Multimedia provided audio. Each of the three stage types has its own small set of actions. On the rooftops, one key swings a web and another shoots it.[3][2]

Release and reception

Sony announced the game in March 2004 as part of a phone campaign for Spider-Man 2, initially exclusive to Sprint PCS Vision in the US for a limited time. The campaign included separate pinball, 3D and text-message games. Sony also advertised a code inside Doc Ock for Activision’s console Spider-Man 2.[1]

Avery Score liked how quickly the controls could be learned. He found little reason to keep replaying the same three scenarios.[2]
