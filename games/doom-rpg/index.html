Doom RPG

The Mars base advances one turn at a time in a first-person RPG built around a phone keypad.

About the game

You still enter a Mars research base full of demons. The change is what happens after you move. Walking a tile or firing spends a turn, giving the creatures around you a chance to move or strike. Credits buy upgrades, and better health or accuracy carries forward from one sector to the next.[1]

Development

John Carmack started the project after trying the Java game demos on a replacement phone. Its directional pad worked for one hand, but he found it awkward for the combination of movement and fire that a Doom shooter needed. Tile-by-tile movement let him keep the first-person view without asking a player to press two controls at once.[2]

Carmack did three stretches of programming and held design reviews. Fountainhead Entertainment carried the game through to release. In December 2005 he described a more than twentyfold performance difference between the modest Java devices they supported and the high-end BREW hardware. Java and BREW builds could share the game's design while making different demands on the handset.[3]

Release and reception

In July 2005, Pocket Gamer reported that id and JAMDAT planned the game for the autumn, around the Doom film. Its Java review appeared on November 9.[4][1]
