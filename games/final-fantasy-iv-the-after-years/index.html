Final Fantasy IV: The After Years

Final Fantasy IV’s phone sequel gave Rydia, Edge and other returning characters episodes of their own.

About the game

Cecil and Rosa’s son Ceodore is at the centre of the story. Players can choose which character chapters to play on the way to the finale. Battles keep Final Fantasy IV’s Active Time Battle system and let party members combine their abilities. The phase of the moon also affects battles.[1][2]

Development

The mobile team approached producer Takashi Tokita while he was working on the DS remake of Final Fantasy IV. A phone sequel could follow quickly after that remake. The team chose 2D sprites to keep the hardware requirements low and retain the look of the original.[3][4]

The separate episodes gave the returning characters room for their own stories. Tokita described working out where each episode fit and how much of the larger plot it could reveal.[3][1]

Release and reception

The Java edition reached SoftBank 3G phones through Yahoo! Keitai on 4 November 2008. The prologue was free, with Square Enix listing paid content at ¥315–¥525. DoCoMo’s i-mode edition and au’s BREW edition had launched earlier that year.[1][2][5]

Square Enix reported two million paid downloads by the time the SoftBank version launched. That was a milestone for the existing mobile releases, before SoftBank players could have contributed.[2]

History sources

    Matrix’s original mobile product page lists dates for DoCoMo, au and SoftBank, and describes the original episodic structure.

  2. Final Fantasy IV: The After Years – Yahoo! Keitai release and download milestone ↗2008-11-04 · publisher announcement

    Square Enix announced the SoftBank rollout, pricing, chapter structure and combat systems. The company reports a two-million paid-download milestone across the earlier mobile editions.

  3. Reflections After Final Fantasy IV: The After Years ↗2009-07-24 · producer interview

    Producer Takashi Tokita describes the original Japanese phone project, including its beginnings, the use of 2D sprites and the work of fitting the episodes together. Interview published after the WiiWare release.

  4. Years After: The Final Fantasy IV Interview ↗2011-04-19 · developer interview

    Tokita discusses making a sequel alongside the DS remake and explains why a phone platform suited the schedule and episodic format.

    Indexes the 4 November 2008 Japanese release as J2ME and lists Matrix Software and Square Enix. Release table checked through index after direct 403.
