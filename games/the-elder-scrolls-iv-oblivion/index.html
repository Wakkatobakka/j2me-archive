The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion

Vir2L and Superscape reworked the Oblivion story for a wider range of phones than their earlier Elder Scrolls games could reach.

About the game

The phone version follows the same broad story as the PC and Xbox 360 game. The route through it is shorter, with small cutscenes linking the two versions. Choosing a class changes how you fight. A knight relies on melee weapons, while an archer attacks at range and a sorcerer can use spells.[1]

Development

Vir2L’s Douglas Frederick described a twelve-month production that had to fit the story, varied environments and a class system into a roughly 200 KB budget. Stormhold and Dawnstar had only reached 22 higher-end phone models. The new team built for both J2ME and BREW and aimed for about 75 models.[1]

Greg Gorden returned as Elder Scrolls design director after working on the previous Elder Scrolls Travels titles. Superscape executive producer Brad Pitser oversaw development with Barking Lizards. Vir2L kept the main series name because the mobile game adapts an existing Elder Scrolls story, unlike its earlier Travels productions.[1]

Release and reception

Contemporary carrier reports place the phone game on Cingular in November 2006 and Rogers by December 28. Those reports name the carriers. They do not identify every handset and launch date for the Java line.[2][3][1]
