God of War: Betrayal

Kratos is framed for a murder in a phone game set between the first two God of War games.

About the game

Kratos pursues the assassin who has framed him for the death of Argos. The chase takes him through a side-scrolling world with enough depth to keep the angled look of the console games. Marianne Krawczyk worked with the mobile team on the story, using the gap before God of War II to explain how Kratos’s relationship with the gods deteriorated.[1][2]

Development

Philip Cohen drafted the design in autumn 2005. Production began in August 2006, and the first high-end builds were done the following April. Low-end models needed two more months. Once the first six handsets were ready in June, the game went to the porting team.[2]

Art director Nathan Leland’s team built a single set of background tiles and used palette swaps to make different environments fit within a few hundred kilobytes, and less on many phones. Philip Cohen said traps, animated scenery and enemies had to share that budget with Kratos’s fighting moves. Some levels were cut back to keep those moves.[2]

Cohen borrowed the approach to finishing moves from the studio’s Casino Royale phone game. Betrayal originally put chests, levers and finishing moves on up. On a phone, a stray press could turn an attempt to open a chest into a jump into a trap. The team moved all three actions to down.[2]

Production artist Jeremy Steiner worked on backgrounds, interface screens and sprite animation. Javaground’s David Frankel describes leading art and animation on his studio’s side of the collaboration. The Java credits also identify WayForward’s Chris Kerlegon as the artist and animator for Kratos.[3][4][5]

Release and reception

Engadget saw the game at Sony Online Entertainment’s Gamer Day in May 2007. MobyGames records a June 20 release on both Java and BREW under Sony Pictures Mobile.[6][7]
